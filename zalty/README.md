## Zalty Mono

Zalty Mono is a monospaced typeface for people who spend more time reading code than writing prose.

Designed around visual distinction, cognitive clarity, and long-session comfort, Zalty Mono prioritizes recognizable glyph silhouettes over raw information density. Ambiguous characters such as 0, O, 1, I, and l are intentionally differentiated to reduce misreads during debugging, code review, and terminal work.

The design philosophy combines the practicality of classic engineering terminals with a small amount of personality. Most glyphs are restrained and highly legible, while a handful of characters serve as subtle signatures of the typeface. The goal is not to draw attention to the font itself, but to make reading feel effortless while occasionally rewarding a closer look.

Zalty Mono takes inspiration from the culture surrounding Lisp machines, Bell Labs, early Unix systems, hacker workstations, and modern software craftsmanship. It is intended to feel equally at home in a terminal emulator, a code editor, a REPL, or a late-night debugging session.

## Design Goals

* Clear distinction between commonly confused characters
* High readability at small sizes
* Comfortable extended reading sessions
* Consistent monospace metrics
* Subtle personality without sacrificing utility
* Optimized for source code, terminals, logs, and technical writing
